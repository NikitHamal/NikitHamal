## Facts and wording
- **Yamapanchak.** Tihar's five days are tied to Yama, the god of death: crows as his messengers, dogs as the guardians of his gate.
- **Yama and Yamuna.** In the Nepali telling, Yamuna gave her brother Yama tika and a makhamali (globe amaranth) garland, ringed him with oil and asked that he live until the oil dried, the dubo wilted and the makhamali faded. Makhamali barely wilts, so the blessing reads as a wish for undying life. Sisters still draw the oil ring and give the makhamali garland at Bhai Tika.
- **Days.** 1 Kag Tihar; 2 Kukur Tihar; 3 Gai puja in the morning and Laxmi Puja on the Amavasya night (bhailo begins); 4 Govardhan puja / Goru (ox) puja, and among Newars **Mha Puja** (worship of one's own body and self) with the Nepal Sambat new year; 5 Bhai Tika (saptarangi tika, makhamali mala, walnut).
- **Nepal Sambat** began in 879 CE. Tradition credits **Sankhadhar Sakhwa**, a national hero of Nepal, with paying off the people's debts. The year beginning at Mha Puja 2026 is NS 1147. Line 14 says *सम्झनामा* (in memory of): a remembrance, not a precise historical claim.
- **Deusi and bhailo:** groups sing door to door; bhailo is traditionally sung by girls (from Laxmi Puja night), deusi by boys.
- **Plurality.** Line 17: in households without brothers, sisters give tika to sisters, and friends to friends.
