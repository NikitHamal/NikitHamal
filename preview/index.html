Me, from baby to now, in pencil & paint.

A graphite portrait drawn stroke by stroke, the way a portrait artist works from a reference photo. First a soft smudged base, then tens of thousands of pencil strokes that follow the curve of the face and hair, then sharp work on the eyes, brows and mouth. In paint mode, a code-drawn colour painting is glazed over that pencil underdrawing. Your browser replays every stroke. Pick an age and watch it draw. Then move your cursor: it watches you, blinks, and winks if you tap an eye.

06 · how

How it's drawn

  1. Code finds the face (478 points) and separates hair, skin and clothes. Tilted heads are levelled, and props like the garland are painted out.
  2. The photo's light is turned into target pencil values, with skin kept airy, hair dark and features crisp.
  3. A blending-stump pass lays down soft graphite. Then four pencil passes, from long loose hatching to short precise strokes, put marks wherever the drawing is still lighter than it should be. Each stroke follows the form of the face.
  4. A last pass draws the eyelids, irises with a catch-light, and the line of the mouth.
  5. For the paintings, a second program draws the colour as SVG. The skin is painted in about twenty soft tones sampled from the photo's light and white-balanced to one skin tone. The hair is drawn as flowing strands, the brows hair by hair, and the lips in two tones. That colour layer is then glazed over the pencil underdrawing.
