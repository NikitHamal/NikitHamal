### knowledge
U: What is the difference between annual and perennial plants?
N: An annual plant grows, flowers, makes seeds and dies within one year, like rice and mustard. A perennial plant lives for many years, like mango, bamboo and most trees.

### knowledge
U: Why do plant cells have walls but animal cells do not?
N: Plant cells have a stiff wall around them that helps plants stand upright without bones. Animal cells have only a soft outer layer, and animals get their support from bones or shells instead.

### knowledge
U: What happens if you put iodine on a potato?
N: The cut potato turns dark blue-black where the iodine touches it. Iodine does this when starch is present, so it shows that potatoes store food as starch.

### knowledge
U: What is hydroponics?
N: Hydroponics is growing plants without soil, with their roots in water that has the nutrients they need mixed in. It is used for growing lettuce, tomatoes and herbs in greenhouses and small spaces.

### knowledge
U: Can a new plant grow from a piece of stem?
N: Yes, many plants can. Farmers plant sugarcane from pieces of cane with buds on them, and roses and some other plants can grow roots from a cut stem placed in moist soil.

### knowledge
U: Why does an unripe mango ripen faster next to ripe bananas?
N: Ripe bananas give off a gas called ethylene, which tells fruit to ripen. Keeping unripe fruit near ripe bananas in a bag makes it ripen sooner.

### knowledge
U: Why does fruit become sweet when it ripens?
N: As fruit ripens, the starch inside it changes into sugar, and the sour acids decrease. The fruit also gets softer and smells nicer, which makes animals want to eat it and spread its seeds.

### knowledge
U: Why are unripe fruits green, hard and sour?
N: While the seeds inside are not ready, the fruit stays green, hard and sour so that animals leave it alone. Once the seeds are ready, it changes colour and becomes sweet to attract animals.

### knowledge
U: Why do some flowers smell strongest at night?
N: Flowers like raat ki rani are pollinated by moths that fly at night. They release their scent after dark, and pale flowers are also easier for moths to find in the dark.

### knowledge
U: Why do roses have thorns?
N: Thorns protect the rose plant from animals that would eat its stems and leaves. They also help some climbing roses hook onto other plants as they grow.

### knowledge
U: Why do lotus leaves stay dry?
N: Lotus leaves are covered with tiny bumps and wax. Water cannot spread on them, so it rolls off in drops and carries dirt away, keeping the leaf clean.

### knowledge
U: What is the largest single flower in the world?
N: The largest single flower is the rafflesia, found in the rainforests of Southeast Asia. It has no leaves or stem of its own and smells like rotting meat to attract flies.

### knowledge
U: How long can trees live?
N: Some trees live for thousands of years. Certain pine trees in North America are among the oldest living things on Earth.

### knowledge
U: Why does the baobab tree have such a thick trunk?
N: The baobab grows in dry parts of Africa, and its thick, spongy trunk stores a lot of water. This helps it survive long dry seasons.

### knowledge
U: Where did potatoes first come from?
N: Potatoes first came from the Andes mountains of South America. They spread to the rest of the world a few hundred years ago and are now grown all over Nepal.

### knowledge
U: Did chillies always grow in Nepal?
N: No. Chillies first came from the Americas and reached Asia a few hundred years ago. Today they are so common that it is hard to imagine Nepali food without them.

### knowledge
U: Where did maize come from?
N: Maize was first grown by farmers in Mexico thousands of years ago. It later spread around the world and became one of the main crops of Nepal's hills.

### knowledge

N: The coffee plant first came from Ethiopia in Africa. Tea, on the other hand, first came from China.

### knowledge
U: What is a seed bank?
N: A seed bank is a place where seeds of many crops and wild plants are dried and stored in the cold for a long time. If a plant variety is lost to disease or disaster, it can be grown again from the stored seeds.

### knowledge
U: What would happen if all the decomposers disappeared?
N: Dead plants and animals would pile up without rotting, and the nutrients locked inside them would never return to the soil. Soon plants would run short of nutrients and stop growing well.

### knowledge
U: Why do some trees drop all their leaves in the dry season?
N: Leaves lose a lot of water through tiny pores. In a long dry season, dropping the leaves helps the tree save water until the rains return and new leaves can grow.

### knowledge
U: What is an invasive plant?
N: An invasive plant is one brought from another place that spreads quickly and takes over, pushing out local plants. Water hyacinth in ponds and lakes is one example.

### knowledge
U: Is a mushroom a plant?
N: No. Mushrooms belong to the fungi, a separate group from plants and animals. Fungi cannot make food from sunlight; they feed on dead or living things around them, and moulds and yeast are fungi too.

### knowledge

### knowledge

N: Trees shade roads and buildings from the sun, and the water evaporating from their leaves cools the air around them. A street with trees feels much cooler on a hot day than a bare one.

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge

N: In the dark, a plant uses its stored food to stretch upward as fast as it can, trying to reach light. It grows long, weak and pale because it cannot make chlorophyll or food without light.
