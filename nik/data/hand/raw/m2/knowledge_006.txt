### knowledge
U: Why do we feel heavier for a moment when a lift starts going up?
N: To make us speed up upward, the floor of the lift has to push on our feet harder than usual. We feel that extra push as being heavier until the lift moves at a steady speed.

### knowledge
U: What is the centre of gravity?
N: The centre of gravity is the point where an object's weight seems to act, as if all of it were gathered there. An object stays balanced when this point is over its base, which is why a wide base makes things harder to tip over.

### knowledge
U: Why do we lean when we ride a bicycle around a corner?
N: To turn, the bicycle needs a sideways push toward the inside of the curve. Leaning lets the tyres push against the road in that direction and stops us from falling outward.

### knowledge
U: Why do ears hurt when we dive deep in a pool?
N: Water pressure gets stronger the deeper we go. It presses on the eardrum from outside, and until the air pressure inside the ear catches up, it hurts.

### knowledge
U: Why do cars skid more on wet roads?
N: A thin layer of water between the tyre and the road reduces grip. Tyre grooves push the water out of the way, which is why worn, smooth tyres are dangerous in the rain.

### knowledge
U: Why does a ball stop rolling on grass sooner than on a cement floor?
N: Grass rubs against the ball and bends under it much more than smooth cement does. That extra friction takes away the ball's energy faster, so it stops sooner.

### knowledge
U: Why are ships built heavy at the bottom?
N: Keeping heavy parts and cargo low puts the ship's centre of gravity low. Then when waves tilt the ship, its weight pulls it back upright instead of rolling it over.

### knowledge
U: Why is it hard to walk on dry sand?
N: Dry sand moves and slides under our feet, so each step sinks and pushes the sand away instead of pushing us forward. We use a lot more effort for every step.

### knowledge
U: Why do heavy loads on a bicycle make it hard to balance?
N: A heavy load placed high or to one side moves the centre of gravity, so small tilts quickly become big ones. Keeping heavy things low and in the middle makes the bicycle easier to balance.

### knowledge
U: Why do athletes bend their knees when they land after a jump?
N: Bending the knees makes the landing last a little longer. Stopping more slowly means a smaller force on the legs, which protects the bones and joints.

### knowledge
U: Why is it easier to push a cart than to drag a box of the same weight?
N: Wheels roll instead of rubbing along the ground, so there is much less friction. That is why the wheel was one of the most useful inventions.

### knowledge
U: Why does a swing go higher when you pump your legs?
N: Pumping at the right moment in each swing adds a little energy every time, just as well-timed pushes from a friend do. Small pushes at the right rhythm build up into big swings.

### knowledge
U: Why do tightrope walkers carry a long pole?
N: A long pole spreads weight far out on both sides, so the walker tips over much more slowly. That gives time to correct small wobbles, and moving the pole helps shift balance back.

### knowledge
U: Why do diamonds sparkle so much?
N: A cut diamond bends light strongly and bounces it around inside many times before it comes back out. The light also splits into colours, so the stone flashes with bright sparkles as it moves.

### knowledge

N: An optical fibre is a glass thread thinner than a hair that carries light. The light bounces along inside it without escaping, so fibres can carry internet data over long distances very fast.

### knowledge

### knowledge
U: How does a periscope work?
N: A periscope is a tube with two mirrors set at an angle, one at each end. Light enters the top, bounces off both mirrors, and comes out at the bottom, so you can see over a wall.

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge
U: Why do we see rainbow colours in a drop of oil on a wet road?

### knowledge
U: Why can we hear someone around a corner but not see them?
N: Sound waves are much longer than light waves, so they bend around corners and doorways easily. Light mostly travels in straight lines, so the wall blocks the view.

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge
U: Why doesn't aluminium rust like iron?
N: Aluminium reacts with air very quickly, but it forms a thin, tough layer on its surface that seals it. Iron rust is flaky and falls off, so it lets the damage keep going deeper.

### knowledge

N: A neon sign is a glass tube filled with a little neon gas. When electricity passes through it, the gas glows with a bright orange-red light; other gases give other colours.

### knowledge

N: Dry ice is frozen carbon dioxide. It is so cold that it turns straight from a solid into a gas without becoming a liquid, making a white fog; it must never be touched with bare hands.

### knowledge

### knowledge
U: Why do fizzy drinks bubble when we open the bottle?
N: Carbon dioxide gas is forced into the drink under pressure when it is bottled. Opening the cap releases the pressure, so the gas comes out of the drink as bubbles.

### knowledge

N: Distillation is boiling a liquid, catching the steam and cooling it back into a liquid somewhere else. Salt and other dissolved things are left behind, so distilled water is very pure.

### knowledge

N: Most plastics are made from chemicals that come from crude oil and natural gas. They are made of very long chain-like molecules, which is why they are strong and take a very long time to break down.

### knowledge

N: Glass always reflects a little light. In the daytime the bright outdoors hides that faint reflection, but at night the room is brighter than outside, so we see ourselves clearly.

### knowledge

N: The moon's gravity is only about one sixth of the Earth's, so a ball falls much more slowly and stays in the air longer. With no air to slow it down, it also keeps its speed.

### knowledge

### knowledge

### knowledge

N: Metals have many electrons that move freely between their atoms. These electrons bounce light straight back, which gives metals their shine.

### knowledge

### knowledge
