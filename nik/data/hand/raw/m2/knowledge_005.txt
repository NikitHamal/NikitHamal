### knowledge
U: dhunga kati kisim ka hunchhan?
N: dhunga mukhya rup ma tin kisim ka hunchhan: pagleko magma chiso bhaera baneko agneya (igneous), tah tah thuprera baneko pathariya (sedimentary), ra garmi tatha chap le badalieko rupantarit (metamorphic) dhunga.

### knowledge
U: granite dhunga kasari banchha?
N: granite jamin bhitra pagleko magma bistarai chiso bhaera kada bhaeko agneya dhunga ho. bistarai chiso bhaeko le yasma saana saana chamkila daana dekhinchhan.

### knowledge
U: dhunga ma tah tah dharka kina dekhinchhan?
N: tyasto dhunga pathariya (sedimentary) dhunga ho. nadi ra samudra ma lakhaun barsha samma baluwa, mato ra kan ek mathi arko thuprera thichiyera dhunga bandai jaanda tah dekhinchhan.

### knowledge
U: marble (sangamarmar) kun dhunga bata banchha?
N: marble chuna dhunga (limestone) jamin bhitra dherai garmi ra chap le badalinda banchha. tyasaile marble rupantarit (metamorphic) dhunga ho.

### knowledge

N: ghaam, pani, hiu ra jara le dhunga lai lamo samaya samma futaudai saana kan banauchhan. tyasma kuhieka paat ra jiw ko mal (humus) milera mato banchha; yo kaam saya-hajar barsha lagne bistarai hune prakriya ho.

### knowledge
U: dhruva najik aakash ma rangin batti (aurora) kina dekhinchha?
N: surya bata aaune bidyut bhayeko saana kan prithvi ko chumbakiya kshetra le dhruva tira lagchha. tiniharu mathi ko hawa sanga thokkinda aakash ma hariyo, rato ra baijani ujyalo naachirahe jasto dekhinchha.

### knowledge
U: What is a geyser?
N: A geyser is a hot spring that now and then shoots a column of hot water and steam into the air. Water underground is heated by hot rock until the pressure forces it out; the most famous ones are in Iceland and in Yellowstone in the USA.

### knowledge
U: Where are fossils usually found?
N: Fossils are usually found in sedimentary rocks such as limestone and shale. These rocks formed from mud and sand that buried dead plants and animals before they could rot away.

### knowledge
U: What is a fault line?
N: A fault is a long crack in the Earth's crust where two blocks of rock can slide past each other. When the rocks suddenly slip along a fault, the ground shakes in an earthquake.

### knowledge
U: Can quicksand swallow a person completely?
N: No. Quicksand is sand so full of water that it cannot hold weight, so a person sinks in, but the human body floats in it and usually stops at the waist. The danger is being stuck, so move slowly, lean back and work the legs free.

### knowledge
U: What is a sinkhole?
N: A sinkhole is a hole that opens when the ground above collapses into an empty space below. It often happens where underground water has slowly dissolved soft rock like limestone.

### knowledge
U: What is an oasis?
N: An oasis is a green spot in a desert where water from underground comes near the surface. Palm trees and crops can grow there, and travellers and animals stop there to drink.

### knowledge
U: How are islands formed?
N: Some islands are the tops of volcanoes that rose from the sea floor, like Hawaii. Others are built by coral, and some were once part of the mainland until the sea rose around them.

### knowledge
U: How does a waterfall form?
N: A waterfall often forms where a river flows over hard rock onto softer rock. The water wears away the soft rock faster, making a step, and over time the step grows into a waterfall.

### knowledge
U: Why are pebbles in a river round and smooth?
N: As a river carries stones along, they roll and knock against each other and the riverbed. Over many years their sharp edges wear away, leaving round, smooth pebbles.

### knowledge
U: What is a mineral?
N: A mineral is a natural solid substance with a fixed chemical makeup, such as quartz, mica or rock salt. Rocks are made of one or more minerals mixed together.

### knowledge
U: Why does a river carry more mud after heavy rain?
N: Heavy rain washes soil off the land into streams, and the faster, bigger river also scrapes mud from its own bed and banks. That is why rivers turn brown in the monsoon.

### knowledge
U: What is a plateau?
N: A plateau is a large area of high, fairly flat land, raised above the land around it. The Tibetan Plateau north of Nepal is the highest and one of the largest in the world.

### knowledge
U: Why do clouds float if they are made of water?
N: Clouds are made of billions of tiny droplets, each far smaller than a raindrop. They are so light that rising air keeps them up, until they join together into drops heavy enough to fall as rain.

### knowledge
U: Are raindrops shaped like tears?
N: No. Small raindrops are round, and bigger ones are pushed flat on the bottom by the air as they fall, a bit like a bun. The teardrop shape is only how people draw them.

### knowledge
U: Why does wind make us feel colder?
N: Our body warms a thin layer of air right next to the skin. Wind keeps blowing that warm layer away, so we lose heat faster and feel colder than the thermometer shows.

### knowledge
U: Why is the second rainbow in a double rainbow fainter?
N: The second rainbow is made by light bouncing twice inside each raindrop instead of once. Some light escapes at each bounce, so it is dimmer, and its colours are in the opposite order.

### knowledge
U: What is a cyclone?
N: A cyclone is a huge spinning storm that forms over warm sea water, bringing very strong winds and heavy rain. The same kind of storm is called a hurricane in the Atlantic and a typhoon in the western Pacific.

### knowledge
U: What is a tornado?
N: A tornado is a narrow, violently spinning column of air that reaches down from a thunderstorm cloud to the ground. It is much smaller than a cyclone but its winds can be even stronger.

### knowledge
U: What does falling air pressure usually tell us about the weather?
N: When air pressure falls, warm moist air is usually rising, which often brings clouds, wind and rain. Rising pressure usually means calmer, clearer weather.

### knowledge
U: Why do deserts get so little rain?
N: Many deserts lie where dry air sinks from high up, which stops clouds from forming. Others are far from the sea or sit behind mountains that squeeze the rain out of the air before it arrives.

### knowledge
U: Why is it so quiet after fresh snow falls?
N: Fresh snow is full of tiny air spaces between the flakes. These spaces soak up sound like a sponge, so noises fade quickly and the world sounds hushed.

### knowledge
U: What is acid rain?
N: Acid rain forms when smoke from burning coal and oil puts gases into the air that mix with rain and make it acidic. It can damage forests, lakes and stone buildings.

### knowledge
U: What is El Nino?
N: El Nino is a change in which the surface of the Pacific Ocean near the equator becomes warmer than usual. It shifts weather around the world, and in El Nino years the South Asian monsoon is often weaker.

### knowledge
U: Why can we sometimes see the moon in the daytime?
N: The moon is bright enough to be seen in a blue sky whenever it is above the horizon and not too close to the sun. It is up during the day for part of almost every month.

### knowledge
U: Why does the moon look bigger near the horizon?
N: It is an illusion; the moon is the same size in the sky all night. Near the horizon the brain compares it with trees and buildings, so it looks larger than when it is high and alone.

### knowledge
U: What are the dark patches on the moon?
N: The dark patches are huge plains of old, hardened lava. Long ago lava filled giant craters there, and this darker rock shows up against the lighter mountains around it.

### knowledge
U: Why is Mercury so hot by day and so cold at night?
N: Mercury has almost no air around it to act as a blanket. In sunlight its ground gets hot enough to melt lead, but the heat escapes into space at night and the surface becomes freezing cold.

### knowledge
U: What is the Great Red Spot on Jupiter?
N: The Great Red Spot is a giant storm on Jupiter that has been watched through telescopes for well over a hundred years. It is wider than the whole Earth.

### knowledge
U: Which planet spins on its side?
N: Uranus spins tipped over on its side, so its poles point almost toward the sun at times. Scientists think a huge collision long ago may have knocked it over.

### knowledge
U: What colour is the sun really?
N: The sun's light is really white, a mix of all colours. It looks yellow or orange from the ground because our air scatters away some of the blue light, especially when the sun is low.

### knowledge
U: What is a black hole?
N: A black hole is a place in space where so much matter is squeezed into a small space that its gravity is incredibly strong. Nothing that falls in, not even light, can get back out.

### knowledge
U: Which is the nearest large galaxy to ours?
N: The Andromeda Galaxy is the nearest large galaxy to our Milky Way. On a very dark night it can be seen without a telescope as a faint, fuzzy patch.

### knowledge
U: What is the Hubble Space Telescope?
N: Hubble is a large telescope that orbits the Earth above the air. Without the blurring of the atmosphere, it has taken very sharp pictures of planets, stars and distant galaxies since 1990.

### knowledge
U: Why don't people in Australia fall off the Earth?
N: Gravity pulls everything toward the centre of the Earth, not "down" in one direction. So people everywhere on the globe feel pulled toward the ground under their feet.

### knowledge
U: Why does the moon turn red during a total lunar eclipse?
N: During a total lunar eclipse, the Earth blocks direct sunlight from the moon. Some sunlight still bends through the Earth's atmosphere, and only the red part reaches the moon, giving it a coppery colour.

### knowledge
U: Is the moon moving away from the Earth?
N: Yes, very slowly. The moon moves about 3.8 centimetres farther from the Earth every year, roughly as fast as our fingernails grow.

### knowledge
U: Why is the inside of the Earth hot?
N: Some heat is left over from when the Earth formed, and more is made by radioactive elements slowly breaking down deep inside. That is why mines get hotter the deeper they go.

### knowledge
U: How do fish survive when a lake freezes?
N: Ice is lighter than water, so it forms a layer on top, and the water underneath stays above freezing. Fish keep living in that water below the ice through the winter.

### knowledge
U: Where is most of the world's fresh water?
N: Most of the world's fresh water is frozen in the ice sheets of Antarctica and Greenland and in glaciers. Only a small part is in lakes, rivers and underground.

### knowledge
U: Would I weigh less on Mars?
N: Yes. Mars has weaker gravity than the Earth, so you would weigh only a little more than a third of your Earth weight there, though your body would have the same amount of matter.

### knowledge
U: Why do planets keep going around the sun?
N: Each planet is moving fast sideways, while the sun's gravity keeps pulling it inward. The two together bend its path into a loop around the sun instead of letting it fly off or fall in.

### knowledge
U: What are sunspots?
N: Sunspots are darker patches on the sun that are cooler than the area around them. They come and go in a cycle of about 11 years; never look at the sun directly to see them.

### knowledge
U: What is a constellation?
N: A constellation is a group of stars that people long ago joined into a picture, such as a hunter or a bear. The stars in it may be very far apart in space but look close together from Earth.

### knowledge
U: What is a meteor shower?
N: A meteor shower happens when the Earth passes through a trail of dust left by a comet. Many tiny bits burn up in the air at the same time of year, and we see many shooting stars in one night.

### knowledge
U: What is the biggest volcano in the solar system?
N: Olympus Mons on Mars is the biggest known volcano in the solar system. It is roughly two and a half times as tall as Mount Everest is above sea level.

### knowledge
U: Is Everest the tallest mountain from base to top?
N: Everest is the highest above sea level, but Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller if measured from its base on the ocean floor. Most of Mauna Kea is hidden under the sea.

### knowledge
U: How do diamonds form?
N: Diamonds form from carbon squeezed under enormous heat and pressure deep inside the Earth. Volcanic eruptions long ago carried some of them up near the surface, where they are mined.

### knowledge
U: How is glass made?
N: Glass is made by melting sand, which is mostly silica, together with soda ash and limestone in a very hot furnace. The hot liquid is shaped and then cooled quickly into clear glass.

### knowledge
U: Where does our cooking salt come from?
N: Salt comes either from sea water that is dried in shallow pools in the sun or from layers of rock salt mined underground. The rock salt layers are what is left of ancient seas that dried up.

### knowledge
U: What is sand made of?
N: Most sand is tiny pieces of rock, often the hard mineral quartz, broken off by water, wind and ice. Some white beach sand is made of crushed shells and coral.
