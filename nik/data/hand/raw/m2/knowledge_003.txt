### knowledge
U: Why do we feel thirsty after eating salty food?
N: Salt pulls water out of the cells, and the brain senses that the blood has become too salty. It makes us feel thirsty so we drink water and bring the balance back.

### knowledge
U: Why can't we see well right after coming in from bright sunlight?
N: In bright light the pupils shrink and the eyes adjust to a lot of light. Indoors it takes a little time for the pupils to open and the eyes to adjust again, so the room looks dark at first.

### knowledge
U: What do red blood cells look like?
N: Red blood cells are tiny round discs that are thinner in the middle, a bit like a doughnut without a hole. This shape gives them more surface to pick up oxygen and lets them bend through narrow blood vessels.

### knowledge
U: Why do we have eyebrows?
N: Eyebrows help keep sweat and rain from running into the eyes. They also move a lot when we show feelings, so they help us express ourselves.

### knowledge
U: Why do we have two eyes instead of one?
N: Each eye sees the world from a slightly different place. The brain combines the two pictures, which helps us judge how far away things are.

### knowledge
U: Why do we have two ears?
N: With two ears, a sound reaches one ear a tiny bit earlier and louder than the other. The brain uses this difference to tell which direction the sound came from.

### knowledge
U: What are taste buds?
N: Taste buds are tiny groups of cells, mostly on the tongue, that sense sweet, sour, salty, bitter and savoury tastes. They wear out and are replaced all the time.

### knowledge
U: Why do doctors tap below the knee with a small hammer?
N: Tapping the tendon below the kneecap stretches the thigh muscle, and a healthy nerve makes the leg kick out by itself. The doctor checks this reflex to see if the nerves are working well.

### knowledge
U: Why does sweating not cool us as well on a humid day?
N: Sweat cools us only when it evaporates. When the air is already full of moisture, sweat dries slowly and stays on the skin, so we feel hot and sticky.

### knowledge
U: Why is sweat salty?
N: Sweat is mostly water, but it carries some salt from the body. That is why we need to drink water and sometimes take a little salt after heavy sweating.

### knowledge
U: Which is the smallest muscle in the body?
N: The smallest muscle is in the middle ear. It is attached to the tiny stapes bone and helps protect the ear from very loud sounds.

### knowledge
U: What does the kneecap do?
N: The kneecap is a small flat bone in front of the knee joint. It protects the knee and helps the thigh muscles straighten the leg with less effort.

### knowledge
U: Why does my voice go rough after shouting a lot?
N: Shouting makes the vocal cords hit each other hard, and they become swollen and sore. Swollen cords cannot vibrate smoothly, so the voice sounds rough until they rest.

### knowledge
U: What is plaque on teeth?
N: Plaque is a soft, sticky layer of germs that builds up on teeth every day. The germs turn sugar into acid that damages teeth, and plaque that is not brushed away hardens into tartar.

### knowledge
U: Why should we not share a toothbrush?
N: A used toothbrush carries germs from the mouth and sometimes a little blood from the gums. Sharing it can pass those germs from one person to another.

### knowledge
U: What makes us feel hungry?
N: When the stomach is empty and the body needs energy, the stomach sends out a hormone that tells the brain it is time to eat. After a meal, other signals tell the brain we are full.

### knowledge
U: What is metabolism?
N: Metabolism is all the chemical work the body does to turn food into energy and building material. It goes on all the time, even when we sleep.

### knowledge
U: Why are some people taller than others?
N: Height depends mostly on the genes we get from our parents. Good food, enough sleep and good health during childhood also help a child reach the full height their genes allow.

### knowledge
U: Why do children look like their parents?
N: Children get half of their genes from the mother and half from the father. Genes carry the instructions for things like eye colour, hair and face shape, so children share features with both parents.

### knowledge
U: What is the difference between a cell, a tissue and an organ?
N: A cell is the smallest living unit. Many similar cells working together make a tissue, like muscle, and different tissues together make an organ, like the heart.

### knowledge
U: What does the skeleton do for the body?
N: The skeleton holds the body up and gives it shape. It protects soft organs like the brain, heart and lungs, works with muscles so we can move, and makes blood cells inside some bones.

### knowledge
U: Why should we warm up before exercise?
N: Gentle movement before exercise sends more blood to the muscles and makes them warmer and more flexible. Warm muscles work better and are less likely to get pulled or torn.

### knowledge
U: Why do we feel pain?
N: Pain is a warning signal. Nerves tell the brain that something is hurting the body, so we stop, pull away or take care of the injury before it gets worse.

### knowledge
U: Why do cuts inside the mouth heal so fast?
N: The inside of the mouth has a very good blood supply, and saliva keeps the area moist and contains substances that help healing. Small cuts there often heal in a few days.

### knowledge
U: Why do we have a belly button?
N: Before birth, a baby gets food and oxygen from its mother through a cord attached to its belly. After birth the cord is cut, and the small scar left behind is the belly button.

### knowledge
U: Why does the nose run when we cry?
N: Tears drain from the inner corner of each eye through tiny tubes into the nose. When we cry, there are so many tears that they come out through the nose too.

### knowledge

N: Yes, fingernails grow noticeably faster than toenails. Scientists think it is partly because we use our hands more and they get more blood flow.

### knowledge
U: What is a hormone?
N: A hormone is a chemical messenger made by a gland and carried in the blood. Hormones tell different parts of the body what to do, for example to grow, to feel hungry or to get ready for danger.

### knowledge

N: Sunburn damages the top layer of skin cells. The body sheds those damaged cells, so the skin peels a few days later while new skin forms underneath.

### knowledge
U: What is the collarbone?
N: The collarbone is the long, thin bone between the base of the neck and the shoulder on each side. It holds the arm away from the body, and it is one of the bones most often broken in a fall on the shoulder or an outstretched hand.

### knowledge
U: What is the tailbone?
N: The tailbone is a small group of joined bones at the very bottom of the spine. Some muscles and ligaments are attached to it, and it supports us when we lean back while sitting.

### knowledge
U: Why does the skin on our knuckles look wrinkled?
N: The skin over the knuckles has extra folds so that it can stretch when we bend our fingers. When the fingers are straight, that spare skin bunches up into wrinkles.

### knowledge
U: How long do baby teeth stay?
N: Baby teeth start coming in during the first year of life and begin to fall out at around six years of age. By the early teens most children have lost all of them and have their permanent teeth.

### knowledge
U: How many baby teeth does a child have?
N: A child has 20 baby teeth, 10 in the upper jaw and 10 in the lower jaw. Later they are replaced one by one by bigger permanent teeth.

### knowledge
U: Why does the heart of a child beat faster than an adult's?
N: A child's heart is smaller, so it pushes out less blood with each beat. It beats faster to move enough blood around a body that is busy growing.

### knowledge
U: What is the hardest substance in the human body?
N: Tooth enamel, the white outer layer of the teeth, is the hardest substance in the body. It is even harder than bone.

### knowledge
U: Why is the human thumb so useful?
N: Our thumb can turn to touch the tips of all the other fingers. This lets us grip a pen, thread a needle and hold tools firmly, which most animals cannot do.

### knowledge
U: Why does the heart keep beating without us thinking about it?
N: The heart has its own natural pacemaker, a small group of cells that sends an electrical signal for every beat. The brain can speed it up or slow it down, but it does not have to start each beat.

### knowledge
U: Why can't we keep our eyes open when we sneeze?
N: Closing the eyes is an automatic reflex that comes with a sneeze; the nerves that trigger the sneeze also make the eyelids shut. The old story that your eyes could pop out if they stayed open is not true.

### knowledge
U: Why does the voice sound different when we hold our nose?
N: When we speak, some of the sound goes through the nose and adds to the voice. Holding the nose blocks that path, so the voice sounds flat and "nasal".

### knowledge
U: How do we keep our balance when standing still?
N: The brain combines signals from the inner ear, the eyes and the muscles and joints. It makes tiny adjustments all the time, which is why it is harder to stand on one leg with closed eyes.

### knowledge
U: Why do our legs feel shaky after climbing many stairs?
N: Climbing works the thigh muscles very hard. Tired muscles cannot control movement as smoothly for a while, so the legs feel shaky until they rest.

### knowledge
U: Why do fingers get cold first in winter?
N: When the body is cold, it sends less blood to the hands and feet to keep the heart, brain and other inner organs warm. With less warm blood, the fingers and toes cool down first.

### knowledge
U: What is the windpipe called in Nepali?
N: The windpipe is called "sas nali" in Nepali, and the food pipe is called "khana nali". Both start in the throat, but the windpipe goes to the lungs and the food pipe goes to the stomach.

### knowledge
U: Why do muscles get bigger with exercise?
N: Hard exercise causes tiny strains in muscle fibres. During rest the body repairs them a little thicker and stronger, which is why muscles also need enough protein and sleep to grow.

### knowledge
U: Why does skin get wrinkles with age?
N: As we age, the skin makes less of the springy fibres that keep it firm, and it becomes thinner and drier. Years of strong sunlight speed this up, so wrinkles appear first on the face and hands.

### knowledge

N: It depends mostly on the shape of the tiny pocket in the skin that each hair grows from. Round pockets grow straight hair, and flatter, oval ones grow wavy or curly hair, and this shape comes from our genes.
